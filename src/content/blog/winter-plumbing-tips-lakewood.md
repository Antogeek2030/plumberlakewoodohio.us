Lakewood winters bring freeze-thaw cycles that stress plumbing systems. A little preparation goes a long way toward avoiding burst pipes and cold-weather service calls.

## Protect exposed pipes

Pipes in unheated crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls are the most vulnerable. Insulate them with foam sleeves or heat tape rated for the application. On especially cold nights, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warmer indoor air can circulate.

## Know where your main shutoff is

If a pipe does freeze and then burst, every minute counts. Make sure everyone in the household knows the location of the main water shutoff and how to operate it. Test it once a year so it does not seize.

## Maintain your water heater

Sediment in the bottom of a tank-style heater reduces efficiency and can cause premature failure. Many manufacturers recommend periodic flushing. If your unit is more than eight to ten years old, have it inspected so you are not surprised by a mid-winter failure.

## Check the sump pump before the thaw

Heavy rain or rapid snowmelt can overwhelm a neglected sump system. Pour a bucket of water into the basin to confirm the pump starts and discharges properly. Consider a battery backup if you have finished basement space or frequent power interruptions.

## Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses

Remove garden hoses from outdoor faucets and drain the lines if you have shutoff valves inside. An attached hose can trap water that freezes and splits the faucet or the pipe feeding it.

## Watch for signs of trouble

Gurgling drains, unexplained wet spots, or a sudden drop in water pressure after a cold snap deserve prompt attention. Catching a partially frozen line early is far easier than dealing with a rupture.

## If you do have a freeze

Do not use open flame to thaw pipes. Hair dryers or carefully applied heat tape are safer approaches, and a professional can locate the freeze and thaw it without damaging the pipe. Once water is flowing again, inspect for leaks that may appear as the pipe expands.
