Most plumbing emergencies start as smaller issues that were easy to ignore. Catching them early often means a simpler, less expensive repair and far less stress.

Here are seven common signs that it is time to call a plumber rather than wait.

## 1. Water bills that keep climbing

If usage habits have not changed but the bill is higher, you may have a hidden leak. Even a small, continuous drip under a slab or behind a wall adds up. A professional leak detection visit can locate the source before it damages flooring or framing.

## 2. Slow drains in multiple fixtures

One slow sink is often a localized clog. When several fixtures drain slowly, the main line or a shared branch may be partially blocked. Root intrusion and grease buildup are frequent culprits in older Lakewood neighborhoods.

## 3. Water heater making new noises or delivering lukewarm water

Rumbling, popping, or a sudden drop in hot water volume usually points to sediment buildup or a failing heating element or burner. Addressing these early can extend the life of the unit or give you time to plan a replacement on your schedule.

## 4. Moisture or discoloration on ceilings and walls

Brownish stains, soft drywall, or peeling paint near plumbing runs often indicate a slow leak above or inside the wall. The sooner it is found, the less finish work will be required later.

## 5. Toilets that run or need frequent jiggles

A running toilet wastes a surprising amount of water. Usually the fix is a flapper, fill valve, or simple adjustment—quick work that stops the waste and the noise.

## 6. Low water pressure at one or more fixtures

A single faucet with low pressure may only need an aerator cleaning. Whole-house low pressure can point to supply line issues, a failing pressure regulator, or mineral buildup. Diagnosis prevents guesswork.

## 7. Musty odors near drains or the water heater

Persistent sewer-like smells can mean a dry P-trap, a venting problem, or a more serious sewer issue. Odors around the water heater sometimes signal a combustion or draft problem on gas units and should be checked promptly.

## What to do next

Write down what you are noticing—when it happens, which fixtures are affected, and whether it is getting worse. That information helps a plumber diagnose faster when they arrive.
